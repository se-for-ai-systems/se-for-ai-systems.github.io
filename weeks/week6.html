Week 6 – Observability & Feedback Loops

Software Engineering for AI Systems

Self-study week – reading, slides and exercises.
Portfolio 3 due Wednesday 3 March 2027 · peer review due Friday 5 March
Portfolio 3 requires the observability design; dashboards and drift evidence from this week may be added in the final report.

Learning Goals

  • Explain metrics, logs and traces, and what is specific to monitoring ML systems. Distinguish monitoring (reactive, predefined metrics) from observability (understanding the system's internal state from its outputs, including questions nobody anticipated).
  • Detect data and concept drift and monitor model performance in production, using proxy metrics while labels arrive late.
  • Define SLIs and SLOs, set up dashboards and alerts as in Site Reliability Engineering, and keep service health and model quality apart.
  • Design feedback loops that trigger retraining, rollback or runtime reconfiguration, including a fallback when neither rolling back nor rolling forward is possible.

Literature

The week follows [Bass et al., 2025] Engineering AI Systems. Read the Bass sections in the order listed. Each exercise below names the section it builds on. Of the lifecycle stages in Bass Ch. 6, this week covers the last one: operate, monitor and analyze, and feed the results back into the next iteration.

  • Required: [Bass et al., 2025] Sec. 6.6 (operate, monitor and analyze: monitoring, incidents, data drift, dynamic model updating, chaos engineering, analysis).
  • Required: [Bass et al., 2025] Ch. 11 (Observability) – from monitorability to observability, and how to design it into the architecture, build, test and operations.
  • Required: [Bass et al., 2025] Ch. 8 (Performance), revisit the “Operation” part of Sec. 8.1.2 (alerts to first responders, SLAs and SLOs) and the “Operations” part of Sec. 8.2.2 (measuring accuracy in production).
  • Required: [Huyen, 2022] Designing Machine Learning Systems. Ch. 8 (Data Distribution Shifts and Monitoring) and 9 (Continual Learning and Test in Production).
  • Optional: [Bass et al., 2025] Sec. 14.1.5 (model performance monitoring in the banking case) – a monitoring architecture and drift measures.
  • Optional: [Bass et al., 2025] Ch. 15 (The Future of AI Engineering) – DevOps 2.0 and the future of MLOps and observability.
  • Optional: [Beyer et al., 2016] Site Reliability Engineering. Ch. 4 and 6.
  • Optional: [Mitchell et al., 2019] Model Cards for Model Reporting.

Exercises

  • Hands-on: Deploy Prometheus and Grafana in your namespace and instrument the FastAPI service (request rate, latency, errors, prediction distribution). Log each prediction as a structured JSON line with a timestamp, request ID and model version, so that any prediction can be traced to the model that made it. (Bass 6.6.1, 11.3.1)
  • Hands-on: Add drift detection on the incoming data (compare live features with the training data, e.g. with a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test or the population stability index), inject drift by replaying degraded or shifted data, and visualize it in a dashboard. Injecting drift is a chaos-engineering experiment applied to your data. (Bass 6.6.3, 6.6.5, 14.1.5)
  • Hands-on: Define a service SLO (e.g. latency) and a separate model-quality signal (drift or a proxy metric), each with an alert rule. When the model-quality alert fires, roll back to the previous model version (move the registry alias, or revert the commit in Git and redeploy from your laptop). (Bass 8.1.2, 6.6.2–6.6.3, 6.5.2)
  • Stretch: Run a new model version in shadow mode next to the current one and compare their predictions before switching. The course books do not cover shadow deployment; compare it with canary and A/B testing yourself. (Bass 6.5.2)
  • Own case: Design the observability and feedback loop for your system, including the thresholds (drift, performance degradation, amount of new data) that trigger retraining, and what the system falls back to (e.g. a rule-based baseline) when neither the old nor the new model is good enough. (Bass 6.5.2, 6.6.6, 11.3) Finish and submit Portfolio 3.
  • Reading check: Answer Bass 11.5, questions 2 and 3 (how to define and measure the health of an AI system; how an SRE analyzes a data-drift alert, and whether it can be repaired in real time). Discuss on the course Discord server.

Notes

Install Prometheus and Grafana namespace-scoped: no operator-based stacks, no ClusterRoles (disable RBAC creation, node exporters and cluster-wide metrics), static scrape targets for your own services, and Grafana dashboards provisioned from a ConfigMap. Alerts cannot be sent out of the cluster; view them in the Prometheus and Grafana UIs through kubectl port-forward.
