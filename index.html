Software Engineering for AI Systems

IT-Vest · 5 ECTS · Course website - Q1 2027

Course Description

AI systems differ from conventional software: their behaviour is learned from data rather than specified, they can fail silently and degrade as the data changes, and with foundation models their outputs can be hard to evaluate at all. Software Engineering for AI Systems is a Master-level course that teaches the engineering practices that make such systems dependable in production.

Motivated by work such as The Five Laws of SE for AI (Menzies, 2020) and Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems (Sculley et al., 2015), the course addresses the complexity of data dependencies, ML lifecycles, system integration, and the often overlooked technical debt that accumulates in AI systems.

You have trained models in notebooks – this course is about getting them into production. The primary objective is to introduce software engineering practices that help organize, scale, deploy, and monitor AI systems in production environments. The course combines a solid theoretical foundation with hands-on experience using open-source and cloud-native frameworks, and it builds on real industrial AI challenges brought in by the students.

What you bring back to your organisation: a reviewed architecture, deployment and data-governance design for a real problem in your company, documented with decision records your organisation can reuse, a one-page recommendation for decision makers, and a working reference pipeline you have built and operated yourself.

Course Format

  • Workload: 5 ECTS, about 137 hours in total – roughly 19 hours per week over 7 weeks, including the three seminar days.
  • Three seminar days (Fridays, 09:00–16:00, on-site at SDU Odense; rooms on the week pages), one per theme: software architecture, the cloud continuum, and observability with knowledge graphs. Each day is led by a lecturer of the day, who decides the format; expect a mix of discussion of the prep reading, short lectures and labs. Every day opens with a welcome at 09:00 and has a talk by an industry speaker after lunch. Read the preparation listed on the week page before each seminar.
  • Self-study weeks between seminars with reading, slides and exercises on MLOps, continuous delivery and observability. Questions during self-study weeks: course Discord server (invite follows by email).
  • A shared reference project that takes a notebook model all the way to a monitored production service, step by step. Each week you then apply the same step to your own case.
  • A Kubernetes cluster where each student/group gets its own namespace. You deploy and operate everything yourselves – there are no shared services.
  • Three portfolio assignments based on a problem from your own company, one per seminar theme. Together they form the exam report. Confidential cases are handled by anonymising or using a fallback case. See Portfolio & Exam.

Recommended Prerequisites

  • Python, including libraries such as NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn, PyTorch or TensorFlow, and FastAPI or similar.
  • Basic machine learning: supervised/unsupervised learning, model evaluation, overfitting; able to train and evaluate basic ML models.
  • Basic software development: design principles, testing, version control (Git), command-line tools.
  • Experience with cloud computing and containers (e.g. Docker, Podman, Azure/AWS/GCP) is an advantage, but not required. You must, however, be able to install Docker or Podman and the Kubernetes tools on your laptop (admin rights needed).

Assessment & Exam

Throughout the course you work on a problem from your own company. The problem is developed through three portfolio assignments, each peer-reviewed; P1 and P2 are also discussed at the following seminar. The final exam report is a revised compilation of the three portfolio assignments plus a one-page recommendation for decision makers and a reflection, and you are examined orally on it. You work individually by default; small groups are allowed, but the oral exam and the grade are individual.

  • Format: Project report (revised portfolio) + individual oral exam of about 30 minutes, based on the project, with an external examiner; Danish 7-point grading scale
  • Submission system: Moodle (Aalborg University)
  • Final submission deadline: Monday 8 March 2027
  • Exam date: Friday 12 March 2027, SDU Odense, room U165 (individual schedule TBA)
