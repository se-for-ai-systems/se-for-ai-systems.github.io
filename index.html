Software Engineering for AI Systems

IT-Vest · 5 ECTS · Q1 2027

Course Description

AI systems differ from conventional software: their behavior is learned from data rather than specified, they can fail silently and degrade as the data changes, and with foundation models their outputs can be hard to evaluate at all. Software Engineering for AI Systems is a Master-level course on the engineering practices that make such systems reliable in production.

Building on work such as The Five Laws of SE for AI (Menzies, 2020) and Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems (Sculley et al., 2015), the course addresses the complexity of data dependencies, machine learning lifecycles, system integration, and the technical debt that accumulates in AI systems. It follows the view of Engineering AI Systems: Architecture and DevOps Essentials (Bass et al., 2025) that engineering AI systems extends the engineering of conventional systems. The quality of an AI system comes from three contributors: the software architecture, the DevOps processes for building, testing, deploying, and operating it, and the AI models together with the data they depend on. These areas have evolved in largely separate communities with different views of quality. To a software engineer, performance means efficiency; to an AI specialist, the accuracy of results; and to a DevOps specialist, how fast a change moves through the deployment pipeline. The course brings the three together and treats the trade-offs between quality attributes as design decisions.

You have trained models in notebooks, and this course is about getting them into production. It introduces the software engineering practices used to organize, deploy, scale, and monitor AI systems in operation. The course combines a theoretical foundation with hands-on experience using open-source and cloud-native frameworks, and it builds on the real industrial AI challenges you bring from your own companies.

Course Format

  • Workload: 5 ECTS, about 137 hours, roughly 19 hours per week over 7 weeks, including the three seminar days.
  • Three seminar days (Fridays, 09:00–16:00, on-site at SDU Odense, with rooms listed on the week pages), one per theme: software architecture, the cloud continuum, and observability with knowledge graphs. Each day is led by the lecturer of the day who decides the format. Expect a mix of discussion, short lectures, and labs, built around the prep reading. Every day opens with a welcome and includes an industry talk after lunch. Read the preparation listed on the week page for each seminar.
  • Self-study weeks between seminars, with reading and exercises on software architecture, MLOps and GitOps, and observability. Each self-study week follows a reading path through the main course book, Engineering AI Systems (Bass et al., 2025), and each exercise names the book section it builds on. For questions during self-study weeks, use the course Discord server (invite follows by email).
  • Hands-on work on your own case that takes a notebook model all the way to a monitored production service, step by step. The data is public or synthetic, with the same shape as your company data.
  • A shared Kubernetes environment where each student or group gets its own namespace. You deploy and operate everything yourselves.
  • Three portfolio assignments based on a problem from your own company, one per seminar theme. Together they form the exam report. Confidential cases can be anonymized or replaced with an approved public or synthetic case. See Portfolio & Exam.

Assessment & Exam

Throughout the course you work on a problem from your own company, developing it across three portfolio assignments, each peer-reviewed. P1 and P2 are also discussed at the following seminar. The final exam report compiles the three revised portfolio assignments, plus a one-page recommendation for decision-makers and a reflection. You are examined orally on the report. You work individually by default. Small groups are allowed, but the oral exam and the grade are individual.

  • Format: Project report (revised portfolio) + individual oral exam of about 30 minutes, with an external examiner, graded on the Danish 7-point scale
  • Submission system: Moodle (Aalborg University)
  • Final submission deadline: Monday 8 March 2027
  • Exam date: Friday 12 March 2027, SDU Odense, room U165 (individual schedule TBA)
